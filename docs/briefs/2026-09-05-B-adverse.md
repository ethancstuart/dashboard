**Publish this one when the book is wrong where it was most confident** — the
seven near-certain calls at 84% come in mostly miss, and low-probability calls
land instead. Rehearsed shape: «6» of «31» hit, Brier «0.247».

The difference from Version A is not the tone. It's that Version A can point at
a construct problem and say *some of these misses aren't real*. This version

## 🎯 Today's Call

Today we're not making one. We're settling «31», and they went badly.

Two weeks ago we published 39 dated claims about whether OONI would confirm a
censorship event in 39 countries, and put them somewhere we couldn't quietly
edit. This morning the resolver read the window and marked them. No human in the
loop, no chance to revise.

«6» hit. «25» miss. And the misses are not where you'd forgive them: we were
wrong in the countries we were most confident about.

## 📊 Top Signal

**We said seven of these were near-certain. «5» of them missed.**

Those seven sat at 84% — the highest we published — on countries where a
confirmed block has been routine for months. They are also the countries with
our *best* coverage, which removes the excuse we'd otherwise have: this isn't a
case of the resolver not looking hard enough. It looked, and the thing we
predicted didn't happen.

Meanwhile «4» calls we put at 16% landed.

That combination has a name. A book that's wrong at the top and right at the
bottom isn't unlucky — it's pointed the wrong way. One fortnight can't tell us
whether that's noise or the model, and we're not going to argue ourselves out of
it with a sample of one.

**We also have a problem with the instrument, and today it works against us as
an excuse rather than for us.** Every call here resolves on whether OONI
recorded a *confirmed* block — a probe matching a known blockpage fingerprint.
Measured on our own table over 134 days: Venezuela runs a 17.0% anomaly rate and
has **never** recorded a confirmed block; Turkey runs 3.5% and records one on
133 of 134 days; Nigeria would resolve MISS through a total national blackout.
The test measures which censors use a fingerprint we already have.

That's real and it's ours to fix. It is not what happened today. Today's misses
are concentrated in well-covered countries, which is exactly where the
instrument works. **We're saying that plainly because the temptation to blame
the instrument is strongest on a day like this one, and the argument doesn't
hold.**

## 🌍 The Board

**What we scored**

«31» calls resolved. «6» hit, «25» miss. Per kind only — everything today is
censorship.

The Brier score is «0.247». Lower is better; a coin flip on every call would
score 0.25. That is the honest summary of this morning.

**What we didn't score, and why**

«8» calls came due unscored: Central African Republic, Cuba, Mali, Niger, Sudan,
Somalia, South Sudan, Chad. A censorship call is only scored as a miss if OONI
observed the country on at least 7 of the 14 days, across at least 350
measurements. Sudan had «1» day. Mali and Chad had none. Cuba had «8» days on
«183» measurements — enough days, not enough looking.

They stay open seven more days for late evidence, then settle as unresolvable
with their counts on the record. **Note what that rule can and can't do: it only
ever withholds a would-be miss, never a hit. On a day like today it is removing
misses from our record.** That asymmetry is deliberate — a block we saw is
evidence regardless of how thin the window was, while not seeing one means
nothing unless we looked — and it is still an asymmetry, so we say it out loud
rather than let you find it.

**No skill score today**

"Skill withheld (1 of 3 batches)" on the Ledger. One morning's resolutions share
a date and a fortnight of weather; that's one observation, not «31». Three
independent batches before a skill number means anything.

We wrote that rule before we had a score. We're keeping it on a day when a
withheld number is convenient for us, which is the only kind of day that tests
whether a rule is real. It cuts the other way too — the number it's hiding today
may be one we'd have wanted to show.

**What would change our mind:** from the next issuance, censorship calls resolve
on a country's own anomaly rate against its own trailing history, not on
`confirmed_blocked`. The threshold is registered in advance and won't be
retuned. To be clear about the sequencing, because it looks bad otherwise: that
change was decided before this morning's result, for the construct reason above,
and it is **not** a response to a bad day. The «273» calls already on the book
resolve exactly as they were made.

## 🙊 What We're Not Saying

- We're not saying this was bad luck. It might have been. One fortnight can't
  distinguish that from a bad model, and we'd be reaching.
- We're not blaming the resolver. Today's misses are in our best-covered
  countries.
- We're not claiming the instrument change fixes this. It addresses a different
  problem, and it might not help this one at all.
- We're not going to stop publishing. A register that only reports its good
  mornings isn't a register.

## 🧨 The Long Fuse

The next cohort resolves tomorrow. Three independent batches — the first point
at which a skill number here means anything — is about a month out. It'll be
published whatever it says, and on this morning's evidence there's a real chance
it says we add nothing.

That would be worth knowing. It's most of why this thing exists.
