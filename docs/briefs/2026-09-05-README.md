# The 5 September brief, written in advance — both ways

**Status: DRAFT. Voice sign-off owed by the owner before either publishes.**

`resolve-calls` runs unattended at 09:45 UTC on 2026-09-05 and marks the first
censorship calls hit or miss. Permanent and public. `daily-brief` runs fifteen
minutes later, at 10:00.

**These two files exist because nothing anywhere drafts the bad day.** The model
writes the brief each morning from the data it finds. On the one morning that
matters most, "whatever the model writes" is not a plan — and a document written
under time pressure, on the day a public record goes against us, is the document
most likely to reach for an excuse.

So both versions are written now, before the outcome is known, by the same hand,
with the same framing. Whichever way the day breaks, the response is already on
the record and nobody has to invent it.

| file | the day it describes |
|---|---|
| `2026-09-05-A-as-measured.md` | the outcome the live data currently projects |
| `2026-09-05-B-adverse.md` | the book is wrong where it was most confident |

## It is the 5th, not the 6th

Verified in code, not assumed. `resolve-calls` selects `resolves_on <=
CURRENT_DATE` and writes `resolved_at = NOW()`. `daily-brief` runs at 10:00 the
same morning, sets its date from the current UTC day, and selects
`resolved_at::date = CURRENT_DATE`. The first brief carrying resolutions is
`/brief/2026-09-05`.

## What must be re-read on the day, not copied from here

Every figure below was measured on **2026-08-30**, when the fourteen-day window
(2026-08-22 → 09-05) was **8 of 14 days filled**. More countries will record a
confirmed block before it closes. **These numbers will move, and the ones in the
brief body are placeholders until they are re-read.**

Read them from the `resolve-calls` response and `/api/calls/ledger`:

- `resolved`, `hits`, `misses`, `unresolvable`, `still_waiting`
- the Brier score and the withheld-skill batch count on `/ledger`
- the list of held countries and their day/measurement counts

## What is already settled and will not move

- **The cohort is 39** censorship calls, made 2026-08-22, horizon 14 days.
  No FX and no seismicity resolve on the 5th. It is a pure reading of the only
  informative cohort that will ever exist, and it dilutes from the 6th.
- **The coverage requirement is 7 days and 350 measurements**, derived from the
  fourteen-day window (`coverageRequirement(14)`).
- **8 countries fail it today** — CF, CU, ML, NE, SD, SO, SS, TD. They are
  **not** marked unresolvable on the 5th. `UNRESOLVABLE_GRACE_DAYS = 7`, so they
  stay `pending` and appear in the resolver's `still_waiting` count. They settle
  on **2026-09-12**. Do not write "unresolvable" on the 5th.
- **Skill is withheld either way.** One resolution batch against
  `MIN_RESOLUTION_BATCHES = 3`. The ledger prints
  `skill withheld (1 of 3 batches)` and no percentage. That is not a choice made
  on the day; it is what the code does.

## The finding that surprised the rehearsal

**The hit count and the skill score can move in opposite directions.** Rehearsed
against the live cohort:

| scenario | hits | Brier | skill vs stored base rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — as measured | 10 of 31 | 0.089 | **−15.6%** |
| B — confident calls fail | 6 of 31 | 0.247 | **+11.0%** |

Skill is scored against each call's own stored `base_rate`. Where the base rate
was *more* confident than the call, a miss punishes the baseline harder than it
punishes us. So the day that looks worse by hit count scores better.

This is the whole argument for never publishing a raw hit rate beside a skill
number, and it is why both drafts lead with the construct problem rather than
the score. Skill is withheld on the 5th anyway — but it publishes on the third
batch, and a reader who anchored on "10 of 31" in September will read that
number as a fall.

## The fallback CAN produce the number. It cannot produce the day.

The handoff asked whether the deterministic builder can publish the miss without
the model. **Verified by rendering it, not by reading it:** `buildFallbackText`
was run against a 5-September `BriefData` and the ledger block spliced in
exactly as `daily-brief.ts` does it.

**The good news.** The ledger line is inserted *after* the model/fallback choice,
so the deterministic edition carries it in full, correctly gated:

```
> **The Ledger** — 31 resolved today, 6 hit · OONI Brier 0.223 over 31,
  skill withheld (1 of 3 batches) · 818 open, next resolves 2026-09-06
```

Nothing there is a number `api/ledger.ts` would have withheld. The exit condition
for 5 September survives a model failure.

**The bad news, and it is worth a decision.** That line is the *only* thing in
the fallback that knows the day happened. Rendered in full, the deterministic
brief for 5 September:

- opens **Today's Call** with a *future* Kazakhstan call resolving 2026-09-19 —
  it prints `openCallLines[0]`, and the 31 calls that just resolved are not open,
  so they are not in that list
- ends **The Board** with "the resolvers check daily", as though nothing had
- says **"driver not identified in today's data"** under Movers
- never contains the words *hit*, *miss*, *resolved* or *unresolvable* in any
  section it writes
- carries the standing line "the model draft was withheld by a publish gate, so
  today carries data without narrative"

So on the single most consequential publication in this product's history, a
model failure would ship a quote-block containing the first public score, on top
of a brief about Sudan's CII and a call that resolves in two weeks.

That is not a false statement and it does not breach the exit condition. It is
simply a brief that does not know what day it is.

**Recommendation:** give `buildFallbackText` a resolution paragraph — when
`resolvedToday > 0`, lead **Today's Call** with the counts, the held countries
and the withheld-skill reason, all of which are already in scope at that point in
`daily-brief.ts`. Roughly a dozen lines, and it is the difference between the
mechanical edition reporting the day and merely occurring on it.

**Not done without a decision**, because it edits the publishing path six days
out and the current behaviour is safe, just thin.
